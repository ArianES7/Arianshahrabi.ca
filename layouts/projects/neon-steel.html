Project overview

From capstone to release

Neon Steel is a third person action game created by BloodFeather Studio. I worked as the Executive Producer and UI/UX Developer, which meant keeping production moving while also designing and building parts of the interface.

We released the game on June 19, 2026. Since launch, the team has continued to support it with occasional updates.

Art

Art cards were labelled, assigned a priority, and moved into In Progress. Automation added an Art Progress checklist with a Finished item. Completing it moved the card to Art Evaluation, replaced Finished with Evaluated, and added the To Be Evaluated label. Once the art producer or I approved it, the card moved to Completed with a Completed label and an Archive checklist item.

Development

Development cards used the same setup. Each card was labelled, assigned a priority, and moved into In Progress. From there, automation moved it through testing, debugging when needed, completion, and archival as each step was confirmed.

Production and workflow

Keeping 20 people moving in the same direction

With people working across programming, design, art, animation, audio, narrative, and UI, a large part of my job was making sure everyone knew what needed to happen next. I planned milestones, set priorities, tracked dependencies, followed up on blockers, ran reviews, coordinated playtests, and organized the work around launch.

Scope changed as the project developed. I worked with the other leads to compare the cost of each feature with the time and people we had available. That let us keep the main game intact and finish everything planned for release two weeks before launch.

I also worked with the art leads and Toronto Film School faculty to plan when combat, tutorials, enemies, weapons, dialogue, and new mechanics would appear. We used the pacing board to decide when combat, enemies, weapons, dialogue, and new mechanics should appear. This helped the gameplay escalate alongside Kade's story.

Approved release scope

Core combat, HUD, save and load, grappling hook, weapons, pickups, settings, hazards, enemy navigation, Chaos Physics breakable objects, vertical level traversal, a player shield, and sword, gun, and shield ultimate attacks. The game also shipped with six enemy types, including three stronger enemies with ultimate attacks.

Deferred features

Codex and audio log pickups, a more interactive Neon interface, an animated liquid Neon bar, a specialized mech HUD, additional accessibility options, and other secondary mechanics.

To make the daily workflow easier to manage, I spent about 20 hours researching Trello automation and building separate pipelines for development and art. The automations created and updated checklists, moved cards, applied labels, handled evaluation and testing states, and prepared completed work for archival. This cut down on manual Trello updates and made it clear what was in progress, waiting for review, or finished.

HUD redesign

Putting essential information in one place

Problem
The original HUD spread important information across the corners of the screen. In a 2.5D game, that could obstruct platforms and make players look away from the action to check their status.
Decision
I decided to group health, ammunition, abilities, and Neon in one place, then worked with the UI artist on the new layout.
Implementation
The UI artist created the visual design from that direction. I then built the revised HUD in Unreal Engine using Blueprints.
Result
Players could check their status in one place while keeping more of the platforms and gameplay space visible.
