Project overview

My first complete game built from the ground up

Eclipsed started as an individual school project and became a complete 2D action platformer. I handled the game design, programming, level construction, implementation, UI, and most of the custom assets, with support from three contributors.

You play as Quinn, who enters an infected underground cave to rescue Zoe from a laboratory. Reaching her means fighting through enemies, managing health and ammunition, defeating the boss for a key, and navigating the full level.

Character and mouse aiming

Rebuilding the character around the aiming system

The character pack I originally purchased was not made for mouse aiming. Its animations treated the character as one complete sprite, which meant the weapon could not follow the cursor independently.

I recreated the character in Photoshop and separated the body and arm into different sprites. The body could keep playing its movement animations while the arm rotated toward the cursor. That made it possible to aim and shoot in any direction while moving through the platforming sections.

Gameplay systems

Combat, navigation, and progression in one level

I built the movement, platforming, shooting, ammunition, reloading, health, damage, pickups, enemy behaviour, checkpoints, and boss encounter. The boss fires acid projectiles and drops the key needed to enter the laboratory.

The game also includes an in-game map with a player tracker, one-slot quick saving, five checkpoints, menus, settings, controls, a death screen, and a complete opening and ending sequence.

Twelve Nuclear Artifacts are hidden throughout the level. The HUD sets ten as the normal target, while two additional artifacts reward players who explore more thoroughly.

Playtesting and final cleanup

Using feedback to improve the full experience

Three playtest forms from March 2025 highlighted the mouse aiming, shooting, animation, damage sounds, sprites, and overall gameplay. They also identified frame drops after enemy kills, reload animation problems, collision issues, crouching problems, unclear navigation, a long artifact route, and the lack of a map and music.

I used that feedback to add the map and player tracker, bring music into the game, and complete another cleanup pass. The final February 2026 pass prepared version 0.9.2 for its public browser and downloadable releases.

Retrospective

What the project taught me

Full-game integration

Eclipsed taught me how much work sits between building individual mechanics and finishing a complete game. I had to connect combat, progression, navigation, saving, UI, level events, and release preparation into one playable experience.

Project structure

If I started it again, I would use Git and regular backups from the beginning. I would also split the large event sheet into separate groups for the player, combat, enemies, UI and saving, and level events so the project would be easier to maintain.
